# Etymology Breakdowns — Skeletal System Flashcard Deck

Short etymology notes for all 227 cards, generated for the `mnemonic` field
in `cards.json`. Organized by category in the same order the deck uses them.
Terms that repeat across categories (e.g. "styloid process" on Temporal Bone,
Radius, and Ulna) are listed once per card, since a couple have slightly
different wording per card (hand-written vs. generated).

**227 cards, 43 categories.**

## Divisions of the Skeleton

- **Axial** — *axis* = "axle, pivot." The central axis of the body.
- **Appendicular** — *appendere* = "to hang upon." The limbs hang off the axial skeleton.

## Histology - Cartilage Types

- **hyaline cartilage** — Greek *hyalos* = "glass." Named for its glassy, translucent look.
- **elastic cartilage** — Greek *elaunein* = "to drive, stretch." Named for its springy fibers.
- **fibrocartilage** — *fibra* = "fiber." Cartilage dense with visible collagen fibers.

## Histology - Cartilage Cells

- **chondroblast** — Greek *chondros* = "cartilage" + *blastos* = "sprout, germ." A cartilage-building cell.
- **chondrocyte** — Greek *chondros* = "cartilage" + *kytos* = "cell." A mature cartilage cell.

## Histology - Cartilage Structures

- **lacuna** — *lacuna* = "pit, hollow" — a little lake, from *lacus*.
- **perichondrium** — Greek *peri* = "around" + *chondros* = "cartilage."

## Histology - Bone Types

- **spongy bone** — also called cancellous or trabecular bone — Named simply for its sponge-like porous look; *cancellous* is from *cancelli* = "lattice, crossbars," describing the trabecular mesh.
- **compact bone** — *compactus* = "packed together, dense."

## Histology - Bone Cells

- **osteoblasts** — Greek *osteon* = "bone" + *blastos* = "sprout, germ." A bone-building cell.
- **osteocytes** — Greek *osteon* = "bone" + *kytos* = "cell." A mature bone cell.
- **osteoclasts** — Greek *osteon* = "bone" + *klastos* = "broken." A bone-breaking cell.

## Histology - Bone Structures

- **periosteum** — Greek *peri* = "around" + *osteon* = "bone."
- **endosteum** — Inner lining of bone. "endo" inner, "osteum" bone
- **osteon** — Greek *osteon* = "bone." The basic structural unit of compact bone.
- **central canal** — *centrum* = "center" + *canalis* = "channel."
- **lamella** — Latin diminutive of *lamina* = "thin plate." A "little plate."
- **canaliculi** — "iculi" is diminutive in latin (small or tiny). "tiny" + "canal"
- **perforating canal** — small canal, looks like a perforation between two canals.
- **trabeculae** — "trabis" like timber or beam, and "ecule" as a diminutive, meaning small. So "little beam" offering structural support
- **red bone marrow** — AKA myeloid tissue — Greek *myelos* = "marrow" (root of "myeloid").
- **yellow bone marrow** — Named simply for its yellow, fat-rich color (adipose tissue replacing blood-forming marrow).

## Long Bone Growth Zones

- **proliferation zone** — *proles* = "offspring" + *ferre* = "to bear." Where cells multiply.
- **hypertrophic zone** — "hyper" = above / excessive, "troph" = growth. Excessive growth zone.
- **calcification zone** — *calx/calcis* = "lime." Where mineral (calcium) hardens the cartilage matrix.
- **ossification zone** — cartilage turns to bone - AKA "bone"-ification — *os/ossis* = "bone" + *-ficare* = "to make."

## Types of Bones (Shape)

- **long bones** — Plain descriptive English, no unique classical root.
- **short bones** — Plain descriptive English, no unique classical root.
- **flat bones** — Plain descriptive English, no unique classical root.
- **irregular bones** — *in-* = "not" + *regularis* = "regular, ruled."
- **sesamoid bones** — Greek *sesamon* = "sesame seed." Small bones resembling sesame seeds embedded in a tendon.
- **sutural bones** — *sutura* = "seam, stitch," from *suere* = "to sew." Small bones found within the skull's sutures.

## Parts of a Long Bone

- **diaphysis** — "dia" = through, "physis" = growth. growing through
- **epiphysis** — "epi" = above, "physis" = growth. the growth at the top of the shaft.
- **articular cartilage** — *articulus* = "little joint." Cartilage at a joint surface.
- **epiphyseal line** — Same root as epiphysis (*epi* = above + *physis* = growth) — the fused remnant of the growth plate.
- **epiphyseal plate** — Same root as epiphysis — the cartilage growth plate itself, before it fuses into a line.
- **medullary cavity** — *medulla* = "marrow, inner part."

## Bony Markings

- **fissure** — *fissura* = "a cleft," from *findere* = "to split."
- **fontanel** — French *fontanelle* = "little fountain." A pulse could be felt there, like a bubbling spring.
- **foramen** — *foramen* = "opening," from *forare* = "to bore, pierce."
- **fossa** — *fossa* = "ditch," from *fodere* = "to dig."
- **meatus** — *meatus* = "a passage," from *meare* = "to go, pass."
- **sinus** — *sinus* = "a curve, fold, bay, hollow."
- **sulcus** — latin meaning "furrow" or "groove"
- **crest** — *crista* = "crest, tuft" (as on a rooster).
- **epicondyle** — Greek *epi* = "upon" + *kondylos* = "knuckle." A projection above a condyle.
- **linea** — *linea* = "a line," from *linum* = "flax, thread."
- **spinous process** — Same root as spine — *spina* = "thorn." A thorn-like projection.
- **trochanter** — Greek, from *trechein* = "to run." Marks a running-muscle attachment.
- **tuberosity** — "tuber" from latin *tuber **= ***"lump, swelling, knob", same etymology as root vegetables and tuberculosis
- **condyle** — Greek *kondylos* = "knuckle."
- **facet** — French *facette* = "little face." A small, smooth surface.
- **head** — Plain descriptive English — a round end resembling a head.
- **ramus** — *ramus* = "branch."

## Cranium - Sutures

- **coronal suture** — *corona* = "crown." Runs across the head where a crown would sit.
- **sagittal suture** — *sagitta* = "arrow." Classically likened to an arrow's path down the midline of the skull.
- **lambdoid suture** — Named for the Greek letter lambda (Λ) — the suture is shaped like it.
- **squamous suture** — Same root — *squama* = "scale." A beveled, scale-like overlapping edge.

## Cranial Bones

- **frontal bone** — *frons/frontis* = "forehead."
- **parietal bone** — *paries/parietis* = "wall." Forms the walls of the skull.
- **occipital bone** — *occiput*, from *ob-* = "against, behind" + *caput* = "head."
- **sphenoid bone** — Greek *sphen* = "wedge" + *-oid* = "resembling." Wedged between the other cranial bones.
- **ethmoid bone** — Greek *ethmos* = "sieve" + *-oid* = "resembling." Riddled with holes (see cribriform plate).
- **temporal bone** — *tempus/temporis* = "time" — classically, the place hair first turns gray.

## Facial Bones

- **zygomatic bone** — Greek *zygon* = "yoke." Joins/yokes other facial bones together.
- **nasal bone** — *nasus* = "nose."
- **lacrimal bone** — *lacrima* = "tear."
- **palatine bone** — *palatum* = "palate, roof of the mouth."
- **mandible** — *mandere* = "to chew."

- **inferior nasal concha** — "concha" because its curled like a shell

## Sphenoid Bone

## Temporal Bone

- **squamous portion** — *squama* = "scale," like a fish scale — thin and overlapping.
- **tympanic portion** — Greek *tympanon* = "drum." Surrounds the eardrum (tympanic membrane).

- **styloid process** — named based on its resemblance to a stylus (*stylos *in greek) + *"-oid" *which translates to "resembling". Resembling a stylus
- **mandibular fossa** — Mandible root + *fossa* = "hollow" — the socket the mandible articulates with.
- **zygomatic process** — Same root as zygomatic bone — Greek *zygon* = "yoke."
- **mastoid process** — *mast *= "breast" + *oid *= "resembling" process. appears breast/nipple-like
- **carotid canal** — Greek *karoun* = "to stupefy." Ancients thought compressing these arteries caused unconsciousness.
- **jugular foramen** — *jugulum* = "throat," from *jugum* = "yoke."
- **internal acoustic meatus** — *internus* = "inner" + Greek *akoustikos* = "of hearing" + *meatus* = "passage."
- **external acoustic meatus** — *externus* = "outer" + Greek *akoustikos* = "of hearing" + *meatus* = "passage."

## Ethmoid Bone
